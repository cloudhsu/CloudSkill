# ISO/IEC 25010 Quality Model Use

## Edition Control

ISO/IEC 25010:2023 defines a product quality model with nine top-level characteristics.

Legacy projects may still use ISO/IEC 25010:2011. Record the edition in all controlled quality documents because the structure and terminology differ.

Quality-in-use is addressed separately in ISO/IEC 25019:2023. Do not label quality-in-use measures as product-quality measures without explaining the mapping.

## Practical Interpretation

### Functional suitability

Does the product provide the functions needed, with correct and complete outcomes for the specified tasks?

Possible evidence:

- Requirement coverage.
- Scenario success rate.
- Calculation correctness.
- Process-flow completion.
- Defect escape related to wrong or missing functions.

### Performance efficiency

Does the product meet time, throughput, capacity, and resource constraints?

Possible evidence:

- Response latency.
- Throughput.
- CPU/memory/network usage.
- Queue depth.
- Capacity saturation behavior.

### Compatibility

Can the product coexist and exchange information correctly with required systems, devices, versions, and environments?

Possible evidence:

- Protocol conformance.
- Version compatibility matrix.
- Coexistence tests.
- Integration failure rate.

### Interaction capability

Can intended users understand, learn, control, and operate the product effectively within the specified interaction context?

Possible evidence:

- Task completion.
- Operator error rate.
- Learnability test.
- Accessibility/inclusivity review.
- Clarity of status and recovery guidance.

### Reliability

Does the product maintain correct service, tolerate faults, recover, and preserve required availability?

Possible evidence:

- Availability.
- Mean time between operational failures.
- Recovery time.
- Successful restart/reconciliation.
- Duplicate/lost command rate.
- Failover success.

### Security

Does the product protect confidentiality, integrity, authenticity, accountability, and authorized access?

Possible evidence:

- Authorization tests.
- Audit completeness.
- Vulnerability findings.
- Secret handling.
- Tamper detection.
- Least-privilege review.

### Maintainability

Can the product be analyzed, modified, tested, and evolved with controlled risk?

Possible evidence:

- Change lead time.
- Regression rate.
- Module dependency.
- Test isolation.
- Diagnostic time.
- Code/architecture health indicators.

### Flexibility

Can the product adapt, scale, install, replace, or operate across required environments and changed conditions?

Possible evidence:

- Product/platform adaptation effort.
- Configuration boundaries.
- Scalability tests.
- Installation/migration success.
- Replaceability or portability evidence.

### Safety

Does the product avoid or control unacceptable risk to people, property, environment, operations, and protected assets?

Possible evidence:

- Hazard analysis.
- Interlock/limit verification.
- Safe-state behavior.
- Fault-injection results.
- Unauthorized-action prevention.
- Residual-risk acceptance.

## Critical Rule

These interpretations are working guidance, not a replacement for the licensed ISO standard or an organization's approved quality model.
